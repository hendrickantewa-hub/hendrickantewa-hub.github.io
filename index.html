Farming and sustainability

Irrigated production, tested water, careful handling.

Onions and potatoes are water crops in a dry country. How we source, test and apply that water is the centre of how this farm is run.

Water

Our irrigation water passed the irrigation grade water test at the NamWater laboratory in 2026, which means it is fit for use on food crops without carrying salts or contaminants that would damage soil structure or the produce itself.

Our water sources are registered with the Ministry of Agriculture, Water and Land Reform. Working inside that licensing framework is what allows us to plan a season with confidence, and it is what a serious buyer should expect from a supplier.

Using less of it

Water applied to bare ground or lost to run off is water paid for twice, once at the pump and again in yield. We irrigate to the crop rather than the field, and a solar powered centre pivot is planned under the Namibian Agronomic Board irrigation support scheme to cut both pumping energy and waste.

Solar pumping lowers the cost of every litre we lift. Over a season that shows up in two places: a smaller energy bill and a smaller footprint for the same tonnage.

How the crop is managed

Soil and inputs

An accredited soil analysis is on file and informs the fertiliser and agrochemical programme. Feeding the crop to a measured soil result costs less and leaches less than feeding it to a rule of thumb.

Variety selection

Mata Hari, Arabella and Irati are proven commercial onion varieties suited to the local winter season, chosen for storage behaviour as much as for yield.

Curing and binning

Onions are cured after lifting and binned for storage. That is what allows staggered selling into the months after harvest, and it is what keeps quality on the shelf rather than in the reject bin.

Seasonal rotation

Onions occupy the winter slot. Rotation keeps the lands working through the year and keeps soil health from being mined by a single repeated crop.

Why local supply matters here

Namibia imports a large share of the onions and potatoes it eats. Every ton grown, graded and packed inside the country is a ton that does not cross a border, does not lose days in transit, and does not send its value elsewhere. That is the plain case for buying from a registered local grower, and it is the reason this farm exists in the form it does.
